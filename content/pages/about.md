I'm Young Hoo (Ryan) Jun, a software engineer in San Jose. I came to engineering by way of statistics, and it still shapes how I work: measure before you optimize, and distrust any result you can't reproduce.

**At Netflix**, that meant building the infrastructure that validates changes to the backbone network before they reach production. I built the API and orchestration layer that runs those validations end to end, designed the fault-tolerance model that makes every run repeatable, and led the config templating for a new production router platform with a team of eight network engineers.

**Before Netflix**, the same instinct showed up at smaller scale. I built a dental clinic's reservation system alone and kept it in production for three years. In Duke's statistics department, I modeled cognitive function on a novel continuous-glucose dataset, work that earned High Distinction, and that training is where my distrust of unreproducible results comes from. As a TA, I taught the software design course that teaches people to build things others can maintain.

**Before engineering**, I spent two years as an English interpreter in the Republic of Korea Army at the DMZ, working with visiting heads of state, ambassadors, and legislators. It taught me to communicate clearly when there's no second take.
